Fourier Series Visualizer

Welcome to this interactive demonstration on Fourier series.

The Fourier series is mathematical concept used throughout physics, engineering (signal processing etc.).
It shows that any periodic function \(f(x)\) can be written as a sum of \(\sin(x)\) and \(\cos(x)\) terms:

\( f(x) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n = 1}^{\infty} \; ( a_n \; \cos(nx) + b_n \; \sin(nx) ) \),

where \(a_n\) and \(b_n\) represent the Fourier coefficients.

\( a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \; f(x) \;\mathrm{d}x, \; \; a_n = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \; f(x) \; \cos(nx) \;\mathrm{d}x, \; \; b_n = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \; f(x) \; \sin(nx) \;\mathrm{d}x \)

Here you can adjust the parameters of a periodic function \(f(x)\) and the program will draw its Fourier series.
Choose a periodic type and adjust its amplitude \(A\), frequency \(f\), phase \(\phi\) and offset \(\mathrm{O}\).

By adjusting the modes \( n \), you'll see how closely the series matches the original function.
Each additional mode adds one more \(\sin\)/\(\cos\) term, approximating \(f\) better.
Therefore the higher \( n \) is, the better. For \( n \rightarrow \infty \) the series converges to \(f\).
